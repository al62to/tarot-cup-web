Privacy Policy for Tarot Cup

Version 1.4 · Last updated: November 13, 2026

This translation is for information only. Only the German version is legally binding.

This Privacy Policy applies to the app “Tarot Cup” and its website at https://tarot-cup.com. For the app, it applies in all versions, including test versions. On Google Play, the app has the package name at.tonc.kaffeesatztarot.

1. Key points at a glance

The app operates without an account, without advertising, and without internet access. It does not sell any data.

All readings are created on your phone. There is no artificial intelligence in the app and no server to which your cards, symbols, readings, or notes are sent.

The app stores your history, notes, and settings on your phone. A copy is stored only by your phone’s backup in your Google account, if you have turned it on.

The app sends usage statistics and crash reports via Google Firebase only if you agree. Your readings and notes are never included.

The app does not ask for your question. You only think of it.

The website loads Google Analytics only if you agree. Without your consent, it stores only your choice in a necessary cookie. When you visit it, the host GitHub stores your IP address to protect the service.

You delete what the app has stored in the app itself or by uninstalling it. If you have questions, write me an email. You can lodge a complaint with the Austrian Data Protection Authority (Datenschutzbehörde).

3. What this app does not include

  • no account and no registration,
  • no advertising and no sale of data,
  • no artificial intelligence in the app: the app puts the readings together on your phone from ready-made texts,
  • no input field for your question,
  • no camera and no photos,
  • no tools for advertising in the app; tools for usage statistics and crash reports (Google Firebase) run only if you agree (Section 9),
  • without your consent, no counting for statistics: in that case, the app itself sends nothing; only for the notice about new versions and for the rating window does it contact the Play Store on your phone (Section 8). With your consent, it sends usage statistics and crash reports to Google (Section 9),
  • no access to location, microphone, contacts, photos, or other apps,
  • without your consent, no identifier with which the app could recognize you or your phone. With your consent, it uses random identifiers for the statistics; it deletes the identifier for the statistics when you turn the statistics off. It never uses an advertising ID or a user ID.

Part A: The app

4. What the app stores on your phone

The app stores everything generated in this process on your phone. I do not have access to it.

History. For each reading, the app stores: the date and time, the type of reading (for example Card of the Day or coffee grounds), the cards drawn and whether they were reversed, for coffee grounds readings the chosen symbols with their place in the cup and the chosen area of life, and the text of the reading.

Notes. For each reading, you can write your own note, for example “What came of it?”. The note stays on your phone; if backup via Google is enabled, a copy may be stored in your Google account (Section 7). It does not appear in the image you share (Section 6).

Rhythm of the readings. Each reading has its rhythm, for example Three Cards every three days. For this, the app remembers when you last did which reading, and the Card of the Day for the day, so that it stays the same all day.

Settings. The app remembers: the language; whether, and at what time, it should remind you about the Card of the Day; that you confirmed at the first start that you are at least 16 years old; which version of the Terms of Use you accepted and when; when a new version was first shown to you; how much of the 30-day period has already passed and when the app last counted this; how many full readings you have completed and on which day it last asked you for a rating (Section 8); which version of this Privacy Policy you last opened in the app (so that it can mark a new version once, Section 19); how often you have already shuffled, so that the hint about shuffling appears only the first few times; and whether the first start has been completed.

The app remembers all of this only on your phone. What the operating system may back up is set out in Section 7, and how you delete it in Section 14.

5. Reminder for the Card of the Day

If you turn on the reminder, the app schedules a notification at the time you choose. For this, the operating system asks you for permission. The app asks for the “Notifications” permission (POST_NOTIFICATIONS) only when you turn on the reminder, not before. These are local notifications on your phone: the reminder is scheduled and shown on your phone. Nothing is transmitted in the process, there is no server for it, and I do not pass anything on. If you have agreed to usage statistics, the only thing recorded is that you turned the reminder on or off (Section 9). The app stores the time you choose only on your phone and, where applicable, in the backup (Section 7). So that the reminder keeps working after the phone restarts, the app may receive the restart (permission RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED). It uses this only to set the reminder again after the restart, and for nothing else. You can turn it off in the app’s settings or in your phone’s settings.

6. Sharing as an image

You can share a reading as a designed image. The image shows the cards or symbols, a sentence from the reading, the date, and the name of the app. Your notes are not on it. The app passes the image to your phone’s share menu. You decide which app you send it to and to whom. The app you choose processes the image under its own terms. Without this step, no reading leaves your phone, except in the backup, if you have turned it on (Section 7). If you have agreed to usage statistics, the only thing recorded is that you shared a reading or the app; the image and the text of the reading are not transmitted (Section 9).

7. Backup by the operating system

Your phone can back up app data if you have turned this on in its settings. You decide there whether backups are made. The backup is handled by the provider of your operating system as part of your account with them. I have no access to it.

The app allows the backup of history, notes, the language, and the time of the reminder, so that they are kept when you get a new phone. The following are not backed up: the rhythm of the readings, the age confirmation, the details on the Terms of Use (accepted version, time of acceptance, when a new version was first shown, and the status of the period), the details on the request for a rating, which version of the Privacy Policy you last opened, whether the reminder is turned on, and whether the first start has been completed. These details belong to the particular phone.

On Android

Android stores the backup in your Google account, in a private area of Google Drive, about once a day and usually only over Wi-Fi, and also transfers the data when you move to a new phone. In the process, data may reach the USA (Section 13). More on this in Google’s Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

8. Getting the app through Google Play

You get the app through Google Play. Google processes everything related to downloads, updates, ratings, and test programs under its own responsibility and according to its own rules. I see only what Google shows me as the developer.

Installation and account. Downloads and updates are processed through your Google account. I do not learn who installed the app.

Notice of new versions. At startup, the app asks the Play Store on your phone whether there is a new version (Google Play’s “In-App Updates” library). According to Google, the Play Store processes device information, the app version, and the list of installed modules and asset packs for this, in order to determine whether there is an update and how large it is. According to Google, this data is encrypted, not shared with third parties, and deleted after a fixed period. If there is a new version, the app shows a notice at the top of the home screen; Google Play downloads and installs the update. The app stores nothing for this, and I receive no data from you in the process. If you have agreed to usage statistics, the only thing recorded is that a check for updates was made (Section 9).

Statistics. If you have agreed in your phone’s settings to share usage and diagnostic data with Google, Android collects crash and performance data (called “Android vitals” by Google). In the Play Console, I see only aggregated statistics and technical crash reports from this, for example with the device model and Android version, but no names and no contact details. In addition, Google shows me aggregated figures on installs, uninstalls, and usage, by country, language, and device, without individual persons.

Ratings and reviews. Ratings and reviews on Google Play are public and display the name of your Google account. If I respond to a review, my response is also public. Please do not share any personal information about yourself or others there.

Rating in the app. After your third full reading (Three Cards, Love, or coffee grounds “Read it yourself” with at least three symbols), the app may request the Google Play rating window (“In-App Review” library) once you are back on the home screen, and after that at most once every 90 days. Google decides whether it appears. What you enter there, that is, stars and text, goes to Google encrypted and appears as a public rating in the Play Store; in a closed test, only I receive it, not publicly. You can delete your rating in your Google account. The app does not learn whether or how you rated it. On the phone, it remembers only two details for this (Section 4). If you have agreed to usage statistics, the only thing recorded is that the rating window was requested (Section 9). In Settings, “Rate the app” opens the app’s listing in the Play Store.

Tests. When I test new versions in a test on Google Play, I add your Google account email address to the tester list so that you can participate. I use it only for this purpose and remove it after the test or earlier if you wish. Test versions do not collect any additional data. I record feedback from testers without names.

More information can be found in Google’s Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

9. Usage statistics and crash reports

So that I can see which features are used and where the app crashes, the app uses Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics, two services of Google Firebase, but only with your consent. For users in Europe, the provider is Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland; Google LLC, USA, is also involved. In the process, data may reach the USA (Section 13).

Only with your consent. At the first start, the app asks you once: “Would you like to help make Tarot Cup better?” You choose “Agree” or “Decline”. If you already used the app before, it asks once after the update. Without your consent, usage statistics and crash reports are turned off, and nothing is sent.

Withdrawal. You can withdraw your consent at any time: in the app’s settings under “Privacy”, using the “Usage statistics and crash reports” switch. If you turn it off, the app deletes the identifier for the statistics on your phone.

What is transmitted. With your consent, Google receives:

  • which screens and features you use, for example: Card of the Day drawn, spread started or completed and which type of spread, coffee grounds “Read it yourself” started or completed and the approximate number of chosen symbols in ranges, reading or app shared, reminder turned on or off, language changed, rating window requested, check for updates made,
  • the language of the app,
  • a random app instance ID and the Firebase installation ID,
  • the device model, Android version, and app version,
  • the approximate location (city, region, country), derived from the IP address; Google Analytics does not store the IP address itself.

In the event of a crash. If the app crashes, a technical crash report goes to Firebase Crashlytics: the place in the program where the error occurred and the state of the device, such as free memory and orientation.

What is never transmitted. Your questions (there is no input field), the texts of the readings, your notes, your history, photos, the advertising ID, and your precise location (for example via GPS). There is no advertising, no Google Signals, no link to Google Ads, and no user ID.

Storage period. Google Analytics stores the data for 14 months, and Firebase Crashlytics stores the crash reports for 90 days.

Legal basis. Your consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and § 165 Abs. 3 TKG 2021 (Section 12).

More information can be found in Google’s Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Part B: The website

11. Website

The website can be reached at https://tarot-cup.com. It consists of ready-made pages without an account, without forms, and without programs that run on a server for you.

Consent notice. On your first visit, the website shows a consent notice. For this, it uses the open-source tool “CookieConsent”. It runs on the website itself and does not load anything from third-party servers. You have the equally weighted buttons “Accept all” and “Decline”, as well as “Settings”. Under “Settings”, you see the two categories “Necessary” and “Statistics”. You can decline without any disadvantage. The website stores your choice in the cookie “cc_cookie” for 6 months. This cookie is necessary so that the website remembers your choice; no consent is required for it (§ 165 Abs. 3 TKG 2021).

Statistics only with your consent. Only if you agree does the website load Google Tag Manager and Google Analytics 4. For users in Europe, the provider is Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland; Google LLC, USA, is also involved. Google Analytics then sets the cookies “_ga” and “ga” with a lifetime of up to 14 months. The data collected are the pages visited, the time, the page you came from, device type, browser, screen size, language, the approximate location (city, region, country), and whether you click a link to Google Play. Google Analytics does not store the IP address. There is no advertising, no Google Signals, and no link to Google Ads. Google Analytics stores the data for 14 months. In the process, data may reach the USA (Section 13). The legal basis is your consent (Section 12). More on this in Google’s Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Changing and withdrawing. You can change or withdraw your choice at any time via the “Cookie settings” link in the footer of every page.

Browser storage. In addition, the website stores in your browser’s storage (localStorage) only whether you have closed the language notice. This is not transmitted.

Hosting. The pages are hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub (for users in the European Economic Area GitHub B.V., Amsterdam, Netherlands; parent company GitHub, Inc., USA). When a page is accessed, GitHub stores your device’s IP address to protect the service against misuse and attacks, along with technical details from your browser of the kind every web server receives. This takes place under GitHub’s own responsibility; I have no access to these logs. In the process, data may reach the USA (Section 13). More on this in GitHub’s privacy statement: https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement

Addresses. The addresses tarot-cup.com and kaffeesatz-tarot.com are registered with Hetzner Online GmbH (Gunzenhausen, Germany). Their name servers answer the question of which number the website can be reached at. In doing so, they see the request for the address, usually from your internet provider, but not which page you are viewing. kaffeesatz-tarot.com redirects permanently to tarot-cup.com via a page that is also hosted on GitHub Pages.

The legal notice under the Austrian Media Act is available on the “Legal notice” page of the website.

14. Storage period and erasure

  • Data on your phone remains until you delete it. You delete a single reading in History: open the entry, tap “Delete entry,” and confirm; this also deletes the note that goes with it. Uninstalling the app or using “Clear storage” in your phone’s settings deletes everything the app has stored.
  • Identifier for the statistics in the app: the app deletes it on your phone when you turn off the “Usage statistics and crash reports” switch (Section 9).
  • Usage statistics of the app and statistics of the website (Google Analytics): 14 months (Sections 9 and 11).
  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics): 90 days (Section 9).
  • Website cookies: “cc_cookie” 6 months, “_ga” and “ga” up to 14 months. You can delete them in your browser (Section 11).
  • Backup by the operating system: it is replaced with each new backup. You can manage and delete it in your Google account.
  • Google and GitHub store the data from Sections 7 and 8 and the logs created when the website is accessed (Section 11) according to their own rules. I do not receive this data and therefore cannot delete it.
  • Emails I delete once your request has been dealt with, or earlier if you ask me to.
  • Tester lists: I remove your address after the end of the test, or earlier if you ask me to.
  • Ratings on Google Play you manage and delete yourself in your Google account.

15. Your rights

You have the right of access to your data, to rectification, to erasure, to restriction of processing, to data portability, and to object to processing based on a legitimate interest (Art. 15 to 21 GDPR).

Withdrawal of consent. You can withdraw any consent at any time with effect for the future (Art. 7(3) GDPR): in the app under Settings, “Privacy” (Section 9), and on the website via the “Cookie settings” link in the footer (Section 11). Processing carried out before the withdrawal remains lawful.

Right to object. You can object at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to processing based on a legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, see Section 12); an email is enough.

How to exercise your rights. Write an email to kontakt@tarot-cup.com. I will reply within one month. This costs you nothing. If I have reasonable doubts about who is writing, I will ask. You can view and delete the data on your phone yourself in the app at any time. For this reason, any information I give you about your readings and notes can only be: I do not have them.

Complaint. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, for example in the EU country where you reside. In Austria, this is the Austrian Data Protection Authority, Barichgasse 40-42, 1030 Vienna.

Phone: +43 1 52 152-0, Email: dsb@dsb.gv.at, Website: https://www.dsb.gv.at

16. No obligation, no automated decision-making

You are not obliged by law or by contract to provide data. All features of the app work without the internet, except the notice about new versions and rating the app, which run through the Play Store (Section 8). Whether you agree to usage statistics and crash reports in the app (Section 9) or to statistics on the website (Section 11) is voluntary.

There is no automated decision-making within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR and no profiling. The app puts the reading together from texts on your phone according to fixed rules. It has no legal effect.

17. Children

The app is intended for people aged 16 and over. At the first start, you confirm your age; the app only remembers that you have confirmed it. I do not knowingly collect data from children.

18. Protection of your data

  • There is no account and no server for your readings; what is on your phone cannot be accessed by anyone from the outside.
  • The app does not ask for permission to use the camera, location, microphone, contacts, or photos. It only needs notifications (for the reminder, if you want it) and receiving the restart (so that the reminder is set again afterward). The only things it sends to the internet itself are usage statistics and crash reports, and only if you have agreed (Section 9).
  • Your emails are stored only in my mailbox.
